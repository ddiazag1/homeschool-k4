## 1. What "standalone" changes

v2 made SOTW and RSO the content spine, which meant the app was a companion to books you had to own. You want the program to stand alone. That's achievable — but not by lifting SOTW's or RSO's text, which is copyrighted and licensed for single-family use. A question bank rebuilt from their content would carry that problem into your repo permanently.

It turns out we don't need to. **The Core Knowledge Foundation gives away its entire K–6 curriculum under a Creative Commons license** — the same Core Knowledge sequence I recommended as the backbone in v1, now free, complete, and explicitly adaptable. Combined with public-domain sources and original writing, that covers the whole scope with no purchases and clean licensing.

**Net effect: the buy list goes to zero, and the program gets *more* legally solid, not less.**

## 2. Content sources

| Source | What it gives us | License |
|---|---|---|
| **CKHG** — Core Knowledge History & Geography, K–6 | Student readers, teacher guides, timeline cards. Ancient civilizations, world geography, American history, civics | Free download, Creative Commons — **use, adapt, share with attribution** |
| **CKSci** — Core Knowledge Science, K–5 | Life, physical, earth & space science. **Includes Human Body units at every grade** — covers our third strand too. NGSS-informed | Free download, Creative Commons |
| **CK Sequence** (Content & Skill Guidelines) | The grade-by-grade scope & sequence document itself | Free download |
| **Public-domain classics** — Hillyer's *A Child's History of the World* (1924), van Loon's *The Story of Mankind* (1921), Baldwin's *Fifty Famous Stories Retold* (1896) | Narrative color and story structure for read-alouds | Public domain in the US (pre-1930 publication) |
| **US government works** — NASA, NIH/NLM, CDC, USGS, NOAA, National Park Service, Library of Congress | Science content, human body and health material, primary sources, and **images we can actually use** | Public domain by statute |
| **Smithsonian Open Access** | Millions of images and specimen photos | CC0 |
| **My original writing** | Every read-aloud script, every retrieval question, every tier scaffold, every activity | Yours outright |

### License constraints we must respect

- **Attribution** to Core Knowledge wherever we adapt their material
- **Non-commercial** — fine for family use; it means we could never sell this
- **Share-alike** — adaptations carry the same license
- **No extracting their images.** We use PD/CC0 images instead (NASA, Smithsonian, Commons) or original diagrams
- **Public-domain classics carry dated framing.** Hillyer and van Loon are colonialist and in places straightforwardly racist about non-European peoples. They're raw material for story structure only — rewritten against modern scholarship, never reproduced verbatim

### What we don't use

SOTW and RSO text, lab pages, and review questions stay out of the content entirely. They appear only as an **optional cross-reference map** — "tonight ≈ SOTW Ch. 4" — so the program lights up with extra read-alouds if you use the copy you already own, and loses nothing if you don't. Citing a book by chapter is not reproducing it.

## 3. The stack

| Strand | Content base | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| **History & Geography** | CKHG units, re-tiered + original narrative read-alouds | 2 sessions/week |
| **Science** | CKSci units, re-tiered + original labs from household materials | 2 sessions/week |
| **Body, Health & Life Skills** | CKSci Human Body units + NIH/CDC material + original practical-life content | 1 session/week |

### The multi-age problem, and the fix

CKHG and CKSci are organized **grade by grade** — a K unit, a 1st-grade unit, a 3rd-grade unit. Followed literally, that means three different topics every night with one adult. Unworkable.

So we don't follow it literally. We build a **custom rotating cycle** that pulls units out of grade order — Ancient Egypt taught to all three boys on the same night, at three depths — and re-tier each one ourselves. Adapting and resequencing is exactly what the Creative Commons license permits. This preserves one-topic-three-depths, which is the only model that survives your constraints.

### 4-year rotating cycle

| Year | History & Geography | Science | Body & Life |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Ancient world — Mesopotamia, Egypt, Israel, India, China, Greece, Rome, early Americas + map skills | Life science — living things, plants, animals, habitats, life cycles, heredity, ecosystems | Skeleton & muscles · heart & lungs · food & digestion · **brain & sleep** · safety & first aid · money & planning |
| **2** | Middle Ages & the wider world | Earth & space — weather, water, rocks, tectonics, solar system | Senses & nervous system · germs & immunity · fitness · emotions · cooking · digital citizenship |
| **3** | Early modern + **American founding** | Physical science — matter, forces, energy, light, sound, electricity | Growth & change · nutrition · sports science · risk & judgment · outdoor skills · finance |
| **4** | Modern age + civics & economics | Engineering, technology & computer science | Rotates the strongest units back at a higher tier |

**Note the fix for v2's biggest gap:** CKHG has a genuine American history sequence, so the American founding comes back into Year 3 — it's the thing SOTW covers thinly and my v2 flagged as a hole.

## 4. Tiers — with your actual boys

| Tier | Who | Demand | Mastery |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1 — Name & Notice** | **Kindergartner, 1st grader** | Identify, sort, recall | Names it, points to it on a map, one-sentence narration |

| **T3 — Mechanism & Compare** | *(3rd grader in strong areas; all of them later)* | Mechanism, comparison, evidence | Explains how it works, compares two cases, defends with evidence |

With K and 1st both in T1, you run **two** question sets this year, not three. I'll author T3 anyway so it scales as they age.

## 5. Session template

Same five blocks every night. Fixed structure is what makes it zero-prep — you open the app and read.

| Block | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **Retrieval warm-up** | 5 min | 5 questions from the spaced queue — last night's, last month's, last term's. Oral, fast, no stakes |
| **Teach** | 12–15 min | Original read-aloud script on screen. You read it; no prep, no preview needed |
| **Tiered check** | 5 min | 2–3 questions per boy at his tier. Tap ✓/✗ — the only data entry |
| **Do** | 20 min | Map work, timeline, lab, build, draw-and-label, or discuss — household materials only |
| **Close** | 3 min | Each boy: one sentence. Logged verbatim |

**No calendar — a queue.** Miss Tuesday and Wednesday and Thursday just serves what's next. Long gaps automatically pull more review forward, because more items have fallen due.

## 6. Evidence base

| Finding | Source | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| Practice testing and distributed practice are the only **high-utility** techniques; rereading rates low | Dunlosky et al., 2013 | Every session opens with retrieval. Quizzing *is* instruction |
| Retrieval beats restudying even when restudying feels better | Roediger & Karpicke, 2006 | Answer from memory before any re-explanation |
| Spacing beats massing; optimal gaps expand | Cepeda et al., 2006 | Items at ~1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 3 weeks, 10 weeks |
| Prior knowledge dominates reading skill in comprehension | Recht & Leslie, 1988 | Knowledge is the product — the reading intervention you excluded, delivered sideways |
| Discovery learning overloads novices | Kirschner, Sweller & Clark, 2006 | Read first, investigate after. Never "discover the principle" |
| Explaining generates learning | Fiorella & Mayer | Nightly narration; 90-second "teach it back" per unit |
| SEL effects real but modest (~11 pct points) | Durlak et al., 2011 | Life skills get 1 day/week, not co-equal billing |
| Activity acutely improves attention in children | Hillman et al. | Movement before the block where possible |

*The core citations I'd stake a lot on; say the word and I'll verify each against primary sources.*

**Why a retention layer at all:** CKHG and CKSci have per-unit assessments, but nothing cumulative or spaced — by unit 20, nothing asks about unit 3 again. That gap is the whole reason this is software and not a binder.
